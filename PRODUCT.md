# Cairose

## Register

brand

Marketing and the sales demo use the brand register. The florist's operational panel uses the product register.

## Users

Florist owners in El Salvador exploring recurring flower plans. Initial segment hypothesis: established repeat customers, delivery capability, and an owner involved in daily operations. A website is not required. This segment is not yet validated.

## Product Purpose

Help florists sell one-time flower orders, deliveries on individually chosen dates, and recurring flower plans under their own brand. Organize each delivery in the florist’s agenda. One-time orders and individually chosen dates never renew automatically. Customers pay their florist. Cairose charges the florist for software, with no order commission in the initial model. Do not imply automated customer payments exist.

The owner reports that recurring plans are a new model for her target market. Treat this as a discovery assumption, not a verified market-wide claim.

## Commercial Journey

Explain the opportunity → explore a demo without registration → see the customer and florist sides of the same plan → request help through WhatsApp → assisted configuration. The founder confirmed she can handle those conversations and setup. An actual WhatsApp destination is still required.

## Brand Personality

Direct, warm, attentive. Concrete flower-business examples before software terminology. Spanish for El Salvador, using voseo. The user authorized reconsidering previous copy, palette, and visual constraints.

## Anti-references

Generic AI-looking SaaS landing pages, purple glows, interchangeable slogans, repeated feature-card grids, internal implementation language, and vague promises. Do not present Cairose as a flower shop or marketplace. No invented testimonials, adoption numbers, urgency, or sales guarantees.

## Design Principles

1. Demonstrate the recurring plan from the customer's choice to the florist's work.
2. Explain the selling model with concrete examples before asking for commitment.
3. Keep simulated demo data visibly separate from real operations.
4. Make the next action and its outcome explicit.
5. Sell through product usefulness and assisted setup.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Implementation baseline: keyboard-operable controls, visible focus, meaningful labels, readable contrast, responsive layouts, reduced-motion support. No additional user-specific needs were stated.

## Delivery choices confirmed by the user

The buyer can choose one occasion, multiple specific dates, or recurring delivery. In the current prototype a multi-date selection uses the same bouquet, recipient, address, and card message for every chosen date; this is stated in the form. Dates can be added or removed before confirmation. Each date produces a separate delivery with independent status. Prices are shown per delivery and as the total for finite selections.
